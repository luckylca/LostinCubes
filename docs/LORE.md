# Lore

## The Fracture Without a Name

The world-tree did not burn in the prophesied ending. It broke during an erased event later called the Fracture Without a Name. Realms survived as drifting geometric fragments where land, memories, and divine laws hardened into unstable cubes.

## The player

The player wakes inside the Rootscar Fragment bearing a rune that no surviving archive records. The mark reacts to broken boundaries and can stabilize, reshape, or consume fragments. Its origin is deliberately unresolved in Chapter 1.

## Myr, the Unwritten Raven

Myr is a small raven-shaped spirit whose feathers resemble torn strips of script. It remembers events that official rune stones omit, but loses one memory whenever it speaks a forbidden truth. Myr guides the player while hiding why it was waiting at the awakening site.

## Root monuments

Root monuments are petrified knots of world-tree tissue. They stabilize nearby reality, restore the player, and anchor save/checkpoint state. They are not copies of any existing game's shrine or bonfire and should have a distinctive block-root silhouette.

## The first fragment

The Rootscar Fragment is a bounded valley-island surrounded by memory fog. Its forest, quarry, cave, ruin, and sealed ring were once a quarantine station. The inhabitants vanished after they tried to extract power from a wounded root.

## Vardr Hrafnroot

Vardr Hrafnroot was built as a guardian, not a conqueror. When the quarantine command corrupted, it sealed every living being inside the fragment and continued guarding an empty ring. Defeating it releases the **Root Compass**, which points toward fragments deliberately hidden from the surviving gods.

## Long-term choices

Future chapters build toward four philosophies: restore the old order, allow the realms to be reborn, gather fragment power, or reject all inherited destinies. These remain thematic directions rather than locked endings in the first version.
